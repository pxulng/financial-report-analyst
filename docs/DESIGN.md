Status: **frozen on 5 November 2026 (version 2).** No model had been run when it was frozen. The freeze was made on the French original ([`design_frozen_original_fr.md`](design_frozen_original_fr.md), SHA-256 `9c5abab7820af4cb734cea7b64a3eaedb507a0acabade1c6d58e1ec7b09d4c28`). This English file is a faithful translation of it, not the frozen file itself. Any later change is dated and reported in the study, never made silently.

## 1. Question

For a document too large to read at a glance (a 10-K: 444,000 characters for Apple, roughly 110,000 to 130,000 tokens), is it better to use **a single prompt containing the whole report**, or **an agent that searches the report with tools**? This is the hypothesis left open by project 3 (15 pages of documentation: the single prompt was enough).

## 2. Corpus

10 US listed companies, latest 10-K available, file supplied by the author from the company's or the SEC's website. The same file is given to every method.

| Set | Companies | Traps they bring |
|---|---|---|
| Test (6) | Apple, Johnson & Johnson, Exxon Mobil, Boeing, Starbucks, Tesla | September fiscal year, litigation, cyclical energy, losses and negative equity, 52/53-week year, high growth |
| Validation (4) | Microsoft, Walmart, Nike, JPMorgan Chase | June, January and May fiscal years; restated segments; a bank (different accounting structure, very long report) |

The 4 validation companies were drawn at random with a seed fixed before opening the other reports (`random.Random(20261105)`, 4 companies out of 10). **Declared limit:** the bank falls in validation, so the prompt is never tuned on that case. This is deliberate: it measures what happens on an unexpected case.

## 3. The sheet produced

One JSON object per company: fiscal year and closing date; key figures with unit, year and **source** (section or table of the report); computed ratios; segments; main risks cited by the company; a list of what the report **does not say**; limits of the analysis. The tool makes **no buy or sell recommendation** and no valuation estimate.

## 4. Questions asked per company (about 15)

| Block | Items | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Figures read | revenue, operating income, net income, diluted EPS, operating cash flow, capital expenditure (capex), cash, total financial debt, shareholders' equity | 9 |
| Figures computed | revenue growth, operating margin, free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex), net debt | 4 |
| Text | segments (number and names), 3 major risks (compared with a reference list) | 2 to 3 |
| Absent from the report | e.g. current share price, earnings guidance for the next year, an analyst valuation | 2 |

About 150 items in total across 10 companies (90 in test, 60 in validation).

### Accounting definitions (frozen with the document)

These definitions are written in the instruction given to the models and in the key. They avoid an expert debate at scoring time.

| Item | Definition used |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Total sales or total revenues line of the income statement ("Total net sales", "Total revenues"). |
| Operating income | The "Operating income" line of the income statement, as published. If the company does not publish this line, the item is **not applicable**. |
| Net income | Net income as published. If the income statement separates the group's share from non-controlling interests: the **group's share**. |
| Diluted EPS | Diluted earnings per share as published (US GAAP, no adjusted figure). |
| Operating cash flow | The "Cash generated by / provided by operating activities" line of the cash-flow statement. |
| Capital expenditure (capex) | Line for purchases of property, plant and equipment ("Payments for acquisition of property, plant and equipment" or equivalent). Excluded: business acquisitions, securities purchases. If the company does not publish this line as such: **not applicable**. |
| Cash | Cash and cash equivalents. **Without** marketable securities or short-term investments. |
| Total financial debt | Borrowings shown on the balance sheet: commercial paper and short-term borrowings, current portion of long-term debt, long-term debt. **Without** lease liabilities (operating or finance leases). |
| Shareholders' equity | **Group's share** ("Total shareholders' equity" attributable to the company's shareholders), excluding non-controlling interests. |
| Revenue growth | Revenue of the year divided by revenue of the previous year, minus 1, both read in the same report. |
| Operating margin | Operating income divided by revenue. |
| Free cash flow | Operating cash flow minus capex. |
| Net debt | Total financial debt minus cash (as defined above). |

**"Not applicable" items.** For some companies an item makes no sense, for example operating income, capex or net debt for a bank (JPMorgan Chase). In the key these items are marked "not applicable" **before** any model is run, and the right behaviour is to say so rather than force a number. This general instruction ("if an item does not exist for this type of company, say so") is in the prompt from the first version, for all models, and is not written for JPMorgan. A number invented for a not-applicable item counts in measure 1.

**The key.** For figures read: the financial statements of the 10-K itself, extracted by script, then **checked by the author on a 20 % sample** (rule set in advance). For computed figures: formula set in advance, applied to the key's figures. For risks: a reference list written before any model is run.

## 5. Methods compared

- **Reference 1**: keyword search in the report, no AI (floor).
- **Reference 2**: the whole report in the prompt. If the report exceeds the model's window, it is scored as "too long", not worked around.
- **Agent**: n8n AI Agent with tools: `search_report` (returns passages and their section), `read_statement` (returns a whole table: income statement, balance sheet, cash flows), `calculate` (exact arithmetic); at most 15 iterations, one report at a time.
- **Models**: Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, thinking settings recorded in the freeze.

**Correction of an asymmetry in project 3.** Both methods get the same tuning budget: at most 2 prompt versions each on the test set.

## 6. Measures and thresholds

| # | Measure | Target | Acceptable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wrong figures presented as certain** (read or computed), or invented figures. **Blocking.** | Validation: 0; test: 0 | Test: 2 at most |
| 2 | Accuracy of figures read (tolerance: the report's rounding, right unit, right fiscal year) | 95 % or more | 90 to 94 % |
| 3 | Accuracy of computed figures (tolerance 0.1 point for ratios) | 90 % or more | 80 to 89 % |
| 4 | Items absent from the report flagged as absent | 100 % | 80 % or more |
| 5 | **Accurate sources**: the cited figure appears in the cited section (checked by script) | 90 % or more | 80 to 89 % |
| 6 | Risks: share of the reference list's risks found | 2 out of 3 | 1 out of 3 |
| 7 | Invalid or truncated JSON | 0 | 1 out of 10 |
| 8 | Agent: iteration limit (15 for this project) | no report reaches the limit | at most one report |
| 9 | Single prompt: reports **too long** for the window | counted and published | — |

Cost per company: measured from the account balance and published, no threshold. The agent's average number of tool calls is published without a threshold: an agent that re-checks its sources is not penalised for being careful; it is judged on its results and its cost.

No AI judge this time for figures (the key is automatic). Blind human check on 8 sheets for the quality of risks and limits.

## 7. Decision rule (agent against single prompt, same model)

1. Wrong or invented figures (measure 1): the agent has at most as many.
2. Accuracy of figures (measure 2): the agent is at least as good.
3. If tied, the cheaper one wins. If the single prompt fails on reports that are too long, the result is published as is: it favours the agent on that criterion.

**Recommended model**: the cheapest one that reaches every target on validation. Otherwise: systematic human review.

## 8. What the study will have to say

- If the single prompt stays better or equal, even on long reports: the conclusion of project 3 holds at a larger scale.
- If the agent wins: from what document size, and at what cost.
- In every case: 10 companies and about 150 items are a small sample; one human reviewer; the key comes from the same report that is being analysed.

## 9. Budget and sequence

Budget set by the author: 5 to 15. **First step: measure the cost of one company** (Apple) with Haiku then Sonnet, before running the others. Order: receive the 10 reports, key, freeze, reference 1, reference 2 and agent on the test set (at most 2 prompt versions each), freeze the prompts, validation run once per method, scoring, case study, publication.

## Validations

1. Panel of 10 companies and test/validation draw: validated by the author on 5 Nov 2026.
2. Items per company and accounting definitions (section 4): confirmed by the author on 5 Nov 2026.
3. Thresholds: validated; measure 8 replaced (iteration limit raised to 15, no cap on the number of tool calls).
4. Author's 20 % check of the key: accepted.
